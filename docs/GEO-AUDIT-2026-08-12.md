# GEO Visibility Audit — 12 August 2026

## Executive summary

Kenya Fund Finder has strong technical foundations for conventional SEO: canonical URLs, an XML sitemap, prerendered route content, structured data, and indexable public pages. Its largest GEO blocker was `robots.txt`, which allowed Google and Bing but explicitly blocked the crawlers used by ChatGPT Search, Perplexity, Claude search, and DuckAssist.

The audit corrected that conflict. User-facing AI search and retrieval crawlers are now allowed on public pages, while private account routes remain blocked and dedicated model-training crawlers such as GPTBot remain disallowed.

## Search visibility baseline

The following representative discovery searches were checked:

- `best money market funds Kenya 2026`
- `Kenya money market fund comparison`
- `Safaricom share price Kenya market news`
- `site:kenyafundfinder.com Kenya Fund Finder MMF stocks Kenya`

Kenya Fund Finder did not surface prominently for these queries during the audit. Competing comparison and finance sites were visible. This baseline should be checked again after deployment and crawler recrawl.

## Findings

### Critical

1. **AI answer crawlers were blocked.** The production `robots.txt` returned `Disallow: /` for a group containing OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, Claude-SearchBot, and other user-facing discovery agents.

### Strengths

1. Public listing, entity, guide, and article routes have canonical URLs.
2. The build prerenders useful HTML for crawlers that do not execute the React application.
3. Stock, fund, news, and educational pages use relevant structured data.
4. The sitemap contains public static and dynamic entity URLs.
5. Private routes use noindex policies and are excluded from crawl discovery.

### Opportunities

1. Build more original, answer-first educational pages for high-intent Kenyan questions.
2. Earn citations and links from Kenyan financial publishers, fund managers, communities, and professional sources.
3. Track AI referrals and citations separately from conventional organic traffic.
4. Keep entity pages current, source-attributed, and explicit about data timestamps.

## Improvements implemented

1. Allowed OAI-SearchBot and ChatGPT-User on public pages.
2. Allowed PerplexityBot and Perplexity-User on public pages.
3. Allowed Claude-SearchBot and Claude-User on public pages.
4. Allowed DuckAssistBot on public pages.
5. Retained private-route exclusions for every allowed discovery crawler.
6. Retained GPTBot and other dedicated training/scraping crawler blocks.
7. Expanded `llms.txt` with canonical links, freshness rules, citation guidance, and interpretation boundaries.
8. Added `llms-full.txt` with entity definitions, preferred source URLs, terminology, and safe answer guidance.
9. Regenerated and validated the sitemap: 517 unique URLs, including the homepage and primary MMF guide.

## Post-deployment actions

1. Confirm the live `robots.txt` exposes the new allow groups.
2. Confirm `/llms.txt` and `/llms-full.txt` return HTTP 200.
3. In Google Search Console, resubmit `/sitemap.xml` and inspect the homepage, `/funds`, `/stocks`, and the MMF guide.
4. Monitor server logs and analytics for `OAI-SearchBot`, `PerplexityBot`, `Claude-SearchBot`, and referral domains from AI products.
5. Re-run the baseline queries after two to four weeks; crawler discovery is not immediate.

## Measurement

Track monthly:

- AI referral sessions and conversions.
- Number of target prompts where Kenya Fund Finder is cited.
- Citation URL and answer accuracy.
- Indexed public pages and sitemap processing.
- Branded search impressions and clicks.
- Organic visibility for MMF comparison, NSE stock price, FX, and Kenya investment-guide queries.

GEO visibility cannot be guaranteed by a technical change alone. The strongest long-term signals are crawl access, original useful content, clear entities and timestamps, reliable sourcing, and independent references from trusted websites.
